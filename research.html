Current

Structure-preserving control of stochastic physical systems

2026 – present

Oden Institute, UT Austin · with Dr. Chandrajit Bajaj

Many control problems involve physical systems with known structure, such as energy and conservation laws, that are also driven by noise. I study how to build controllers, both model-based and learned, that keep that structure instead of discarding it.

  • Neural IDA-PBC for stochastic systems (current focus). Extending robust Neural IDA-PBC from deterministic to stochastic port-Hamiltonian systems, using generator-based Lyapunov conditions to account for both the noise and the neural network's approximation error.
  • Stochastic port-Hamiltonian benchmarks. Windy pendulum, cart-pole, and acrobot environments written in (q, p) coordinates with state-dependent wind noise and a stochastic Heun integrator for the Stratonovich solution.
  • Learned vs. physics-based control. A controlled comparison of PHAST's Energy–Casimir controller against continuous-time RL (CT-SAC, CT-TD3) under the same partial observations and an explicit information budget.
  • Structure-preserving continuous-time RL. Recasting RL in stochastic environments as stochastic optimal control, and developing an RL algorithm that respects the underlying Hamiltonian structure.

Past

Multi-muon showers in the NOvA Far Detector

2025 – 2026

Center for Particles and Fields, UT Austin · with Dr. Karol Lang

Cosmic-ray air showers can send many muons through the NOvA Far Detector at once. I developed a timing- and angle-based clustering algorithm to pick these multi-muon events out of the cosmic pulser data stream, as a first step toward studying how their rate varies with the seasons.

Machine learning for energy reconstruction at the NOvA Test Beam

2025 – 2026

Center for Particles and Fields, UT Austin · with Dr. Karol Lang

I benchmarked machine-learning models for reconstructing electron energies and helped validate the MLP energy regressor that is now part of NOvASoft. I also designed a dataset-flattening scheme to keep training from being biased toward peaks in the energy spectrum.
